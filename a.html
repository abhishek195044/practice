Rabindranath Tagore

Poet • Philosopher • Nobel Laureate • Visionary

Early Life

Rabindranath Tagore was born on 7 May 1861 in Calcutta (now Kolkata), India. He belonged to a cultured and influential Bengali family. From an early age, Tagore showed extraordinary talent in poetry, music, and literature.

Literary Career

Tagore reshaped Bengali literature and music. He wrote poems, novels, short stories, plays, and essays. His most famous work, Gitanjali, earned him the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913, making him the first Asian to receive this honor.

Education & Visva-Bharati

Rabindranath Tagore believed in education that connects humans with nature. He founded Visva-Bharati University at Santiniketan, promoting global learning, creativity, and cultural harmony.

National Anthems

Tagore composed the national anthems of two countries: India – Jana Gana Mana and Bangladesh – Amar Sonar Bangla. This achievement is unique in world history.

“Where the mind is without fear and the head is held high, where knowledge is free…”

Death & Legacy

Rabindranath Tagore passed away on 7 August 1941. His ideas of freedom, humanity, and universal brotherhood continue to inspire millions across the world.
